## Slide 4: The challenge: Minimizing thermal resistance

- The challenge: Minimizing thermal resistance
- At flow rates corresponding to Reynolds numbers below 2,300, the flow is laminar which creates significant thermal resistance in the fluid
- In the region with more developed turbulence  (2800+ Reynolds) the resistance in the fluid is minimal
- In most systems today, the brine fluid flow rate varies across the season. This means a significant part of the running hours is in the laminar state (<2,300 Reynolds)
- High flow rates generate high pressuredrop

## Slide 21: Optimal fin design

- Optimal fin design
- Trade-off between:
- Enhanced heat transfer.
- Trigger an unsteady flow at as low Re as possible.
- Unfavorable increase in pressure drop due to fins.
- Not increase pressure drop too much at Re>2300.
- Max about +10% vs. smooth pipe or net gain of fins diminishes.
- Manufacturing costs.
- Feasible and cheap to manufacture.
- → Suitable fin design promotes unsteady flow at Re < 2300, (decrease convective heat transfer resistance) without significantly increasing pressure drop at Re > 2300

## Slide 22: Evaluation method

- Evaluation method
- We use Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations
- Fully resolved 3D Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS).
- + Directly solving fluid + energy conservation equations.
- + No models needed for turbulence or heat transfer.
- -  Requires high spatial and temporal resolution   to resolve all scales (high computational cost).
- Simulation input:
- Reynolds number (flow rate)
- Prandtl number (brine thermal diffusivity)
- Pipe geometry, parametrized (fin parameters give geometry automatically)
- Output:
- Nusselt number (convective heat transfer rate)
- Friction factor (pressure drop coefficient)
